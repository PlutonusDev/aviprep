Writing for AviPrep

This guide covers how we write questions, explanations and diagrams, how to tag your work to the Part 61 MOS, and how points are worked out. Stick to it and your work will get through review and onto the platform faster.

Section 2.3 of your agreement

Everything you send must be your own work

When you submit content, you’re promising that it’s original, that it doesn’t infringe anyone else’s intellectual property, and that it doesn’t break CASA exam confidentiality. In practice, that rules out three things:

  • Copyrighted text

    Passages from textbooks, study guides, websites or other question banks, even if you reword them.

  • Recalled CASA exam questions

    Anything you or anyone else remembers from a live exam, even with the numbers or wording changed.

  • Other people’s diagrams

    Charts, figures and photos you didn’t make yourself. Redraw them from scratch instead.

Breaching this is grounds for ending the agreement straight away (section 7.2). If you’re not sure whether something counts, ask us before you use it.

Questions

A good question tests one idea, reads like the real exam and can’t be answered just by spotting how it was written. Students should get it right because they understand the topic, and wrong because they don’t.

  • One idea per question

    If you need “and” to describe what it tests, split it into two.

  • Four options, one answer

    CASA uses four. The editor allows two to six, but only one can be right.

  • Make the wrong answers tempting

    Base them on real mistakes: wrong units, reversed rules, the step people skip.

  • Don’t give it away

    Keep options a similar length and grammar, so the longest answer isn’t always the right one.

  • Options get shuffled

    Never write “option A” or “both B and C”. “All of the above” locks the order, so use it rarely.

  • Say it positively

    Avoid negatives. If you must, write NOT in capitals so nobody misses it.

  • Keep the stem tight

    Under 400 characters. Give the numbers they need and nothing else.

  • Write for the licence

    A CPL question should go deeper than a PPL one. Match the level of the subject.

  • Set topic and difficulty

    Pick the topic that fits and be honest about difficulty: easy, medium or hard.

  • Use Australian terms

    AMSL, AGL, hPa, kt, NM, ERSA, AIP. Write numbers and units the way pilots read them.

Needs work

Which of the following is not something that doesn’t affect density altitude?

Temperature
Humidity
Pressure, temperature and humidity all affect density altitude
Both A and B

Double negative, a giveaway long answer, and a letter reference that breaks when the options shuffle.

Better

An aerodrome at 2,000 ft AMSL has a QNH of 1013 hPa and a temperature of 35°C. Compared with a standard day, the aircraft’s take-off distance will be

longer, because density altitude is higher
shorter, because density altitude is lower
longer, because pressure altitude is higher
unchanged, because QNH is standard

One idea, real numbers, and every wrong answer is a mistake students actually make.

Tagging to the Part 61 MOS

Section 1.2 of your agreement says all content must be strictly mapped to the current CASA Part 61 Manual of Standards. We do that by linking every question to the Schedule 3 item it actually tests. It’s how we show students and flight schools that the question bank covers the syllabus, so the links need to be accurate, not just close.

CNVCUnitCPL navigation
2.5SubtopicPilot navigation
2.5.3ElementGiven a track and data…
(d)ItemRevise estimates or ETA using latest ground speed

You’ll see items written as CNVC 2.5.3(d). You always link to the item, the most specific level.

  1. Write the question first

    The editor suggests MOS items from your wording, so finish the stem, options and explanation before you tag.

  2. Read the suggestions

    You’ll see up to three items with a match percentage. A high match means similar wording, not a correct link, so read the item before you click Link.

  3. Choose one primary item

    This is the item the question tests most directly. The first item you link becomes primary, and the star moves it. A question can’t be published without one.

  4. Add secondary items sparingly

    Only link another item if the question genuinely assesses it too. Most questions need none, and loose links make our coverage look better than it is.

  5. Search when suggestions miss

    Search by reference, like “2.5.3”, or by words, like “ground speed”. Only items from the subject’s own units can be linked.

  6. Match the verb

    If the item says calculate, the question should make students calculate. A recall question belongs on an explain or describe item.

When the MOS changes

CASA updates the MOS from time to time. We load each update without breaking your links, but if an item you linked has been reworded, it will show “Reworded in the latest MOS”. Read the new wording, then click Still fits or link the right item instead. Content that falls out of date can be archived, and archived content stops earning points (section 3.6).

03

Explanations

Students read the explanation straight after answering, often after getting the question wrong. Use it to teach them why, rather than just telling them they missed it.

Example

A 20 kt headwind, TAS 120 kt. How long to fly 50 NM?

130 minutes. A headwind slows you over the ground, so your groundspeed is 100 kt.

2GS = 120 − 20 = 100 kt. Time = 50 NM ÷ 100 kt = 0.5 hr = 30 min.

3If you got 21 minutes, you added the wind instead of subtracting it.

Reference  Part 61 MOS, CNVC 2.5.3(d) (goes in the reference field)

1

Answer first

Open with the right answer and the reason it’s right.

2

Show the working

For calculations, write every step with units.

3

Name the trap

Explain the most tempting wrong answer.

4

Cite the source

Put it in the reference field, not the explanation.

Plain text only

Explanations show as a single paragraph. Line breaks, bullets and markdown won’t appear, so write in short, full sentences. Two to five is usually right. Symbols like °, −, ÷ and × are fine.

04

Charts and diagrams

Most students study on their phones. If a label can’t be read on a small screen, it can’t be read at all, so design every image for the smallest screen first.

Format
PNG

For diagrams and charts. JPG or WebP for photos.

Width
1600 px

At least. Export at 2× if your tool allows.

File size
Under 1 MB

Uploads cap at 10 MB, but smaller loads faster.

Text
16 px+

At 1600 px wide, so labels survive on a phone.

  • Draw it yourself

    Redraw anything from a textbook, AIP or ERSA. Don’t scan or screenshot copyrighted material.

  • Crop to what matters

    Show only the part of the chart the question needs, with a little context around it.

  • White background

    No transparency, borders or drop shadows.

  • Don’t lean on colour alone

    Label lines and areas as well, so they still work in greyscale or for colour-blind students.

  • No watermarks or logos

    Including your own.

  • Name files clearly

    Like cnvc-wind-triangle-01.png, and send a one-line description with each.

How points work

Every piece of published content earns points. Your share of a subject’s royalty pool depends on how many active points you hold in that subject compared with everyone else.

1point

Standard question

A multiple-choice question with a clear explanation.

  • Recall or single-step reasoning
  • Rules, definitions, procedures
  • Most of the question bank
3points

Complex question

Takes real work to write and to answer.

  • Chart or diagram work
  • Multi-step calculations
  • Includes an original image
10points

Lesson

A full piece of teaching on a topic.

  • Theory lesson or written chapter
  • Video lesson
  • Covers its MOS items end to end

Points count once your work is published. Drafts and content waiting for review don’t earn yet.

We set the points when we publish. If you think something deserves a higher tier, tell us when you submit it.

Content only earns while it’s live. If it’s archived or goes out of date, its points drop out of the pool.

Each month, 25% of a subject’s net revenue is shared between contributors by their active points in that subject.

Before you submit

  • It’s my own work, with no copied text or recalled exam questions
  • There’s exactly one correct answer
  • The wrong answers are believable
  • Nothing refers to an option’s letter or position
  • The explanation leads with the answer and why
  • Calculations show every step, with units
  • The reference field has a source
  • The primary MOS item is the one it really tests
  • Images are original and readable on a phone
  • I’ve checked it against the current rules
